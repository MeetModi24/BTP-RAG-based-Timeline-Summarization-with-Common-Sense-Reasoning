357. LETTER TO NAZIR AHMAD
SEVAGRAM, WARDHA, C.P.,
July 20, 1942
DEAR FAKIR SAHEB,
I have your kind letter. There is no question of my or the
Congress's changing attitude if doubts are removed. Both are
ready at any moment to settle with the League on the usual
terms, i. e., arbitration in the absence of agreement.
For myself what I have said is that I would accept Pakistan
if I know its contents and if it satisfied my sense of justice. You
must have seen Q. A.'s reply to my note. His definition of Paki-
stan must be unacceptable to you as it is to me.
If an honourable understanding can be effected many things
are, no doubt, possible. The best of possibilities is inexhaustible.
The rest of your letter does not call for any answer.
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
From a copy: Gandhi-Sapru Papers. Courtesy: National Library, Calcutta
