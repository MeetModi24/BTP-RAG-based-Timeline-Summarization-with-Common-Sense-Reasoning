249. LETTER TO MAGANLAL GANDHI
[ON THE TRAIN TO BOMBAY,]
April 23, 1918
CHI. MAGANLAL,
I hear from Ba that Santok and you have been arguing at
each other and that you looked sad. I don't wish this ever to
happen to you. Be patient in taking Santok with you. Impa-
tience shows absence of love. It is enough if we refuse to help the
other party in doing wrong. Your worrying is consuming you and
it bars your progress. You should now come out of this state of
mind.
All impressions of sense, O son of Kunti,
Are hot or cold, give pleasure or pain;
They but come and pass, ever fleeting,
Bear them unmoved, O Bharata.
Think on this verse and let it sink into your soul. It is a
very powerful one and it has brought, to me at any rate, peace in
moments of great anxiety. Use Santok's services in [settling] the
family quarrels of Bhupatrai. They can and must be brought to
an end.
From the Gujarati original in Gandhiji's hand: C. W. 5983. Courtesy:
Radhabehn Choudhri; also Mahadevbhaini Diary, Vol. IV
