62. LETTER TO G. N. COLLINS
[May 1, 1931]
DEAR MR. COLLINS,
I had hoped to be able to write to you about the various cases
I had in mind. But I am sorry I am not yet ready. I write
this therefore purely about Mr. Rajwade's case.
You told me he was not discharged because he had incited
to violence. I have now got the certified record of the case and the
writing on which the case was based. The charge-sheet has 'com-
mitting an act which is to the prejudice of good order and of
public safety and evading arrest'. There is no violence here.
I have read the writing in the original Marathi. It is an
unvarnished report of the events. So far as I can see, there is
not a trace of incitement to violence in the report. In my opi-
nion, therefore, the case clearly falls within the Settlement.
If there is any other writing placed before the court you will
please let me have it. If there is nothing more, I hope Mr.
Rajwade will be discharged without delay.
Yours sincerely,
A.I.C.C. File No. 16-C., 1931. Courtesy: Nehru Memorial Museum and
Library
