322. LETTER TO C. F. ANDREWS
AHMEDABAD,
August 15, 1931
I know how deeply grieved you must have been to learn
that after all I could not sail by the Mooltan. I can see as clear as
daylight that it was God's will that I should not go. I made Hercu-
lean efforts to be able to go, but it was no use. I do not write
anything more because the cables will have told you all. This is
merely to tell you that in coming to the decision I had Sastri,
Polak and above all Muriel in my mind. But duty knows no
personal ties. Has it not been my lot often to disappoint dear-
est friends? The thought too of what it must have meant to Lord
Irwin was almost too much for me to bear. But the voice within
294
was peremptory, and so when the Viceroy's wire came, a great
weight was lifted off my back.
I am not writing separately to the other friends except
Muriel. Will you please share this with them?
C. F. ANDREWS, ESQ.
