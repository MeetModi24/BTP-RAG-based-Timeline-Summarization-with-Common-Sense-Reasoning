331. LETTER TO RAMDAS GANDHI
August 15, 1927
These smaller floods come to warn us. There will surely be
the final deluge. None need doubt it. The magic of this illusory
world makes a man forget all his sorrows. This is doubtless an
advantage but that man wins who, mindful of the deluge and
persuaded of the transience not only of himself but of the world,
lives his life in a detached way. To gain this mastery constitutes
man's highest achievement. Truly speaking, all of us are con-
demned to death the moment we are born, and yet why do we
all-the young, the old and the children-drown ourselves in sen-
sual pleasures? It is obvious that we do revel in them, but we
should always ask ourselves the above question; at the time of
such floods, we should ask it with all the more earnestness. Then,
one day, the answer will spring from within. The intellect pro-
vides the answer even today: "Oh man, give up all trace of
passion." But as long as the call does not come from the heart,
the poor intellect is ineffectual.
[From Gujarati]
