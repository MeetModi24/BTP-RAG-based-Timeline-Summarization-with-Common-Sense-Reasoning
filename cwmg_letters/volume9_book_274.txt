274. LETTER TO LORD AMPTHILL
[LONDON,]
September 18, 1909
MY LORD,
Mr. Hajee Habib and I cannot sufficiently thank Your Lordship
for all you have done and are doing for British Indians in the Transvaal.
Reluctant as I am to trouble Your Lordship at the time of your
well-earned holiday, I feel that I am in duty bound to pass on to you a
copy of the letter sent to Lord Crewe as also the substance of the
interview with Lord Crewe. I thought it better to reduce it to writing.
If Sir George Farrar's active sympathy can be enlisted, I have no
doubt that, even though General Smuts may send an unfavourable
reply to Lord Crewe, he will have to listen to Sir George.
If the reply from General Smuts is unfavourable, I hardly think
it will be possible for Mr. Hajee Habib and me to leave for South
Africa. I feel that it will be necessary to take up some public activity
here before our departure.
We hope that you will have an enjoyable holiday and that you
will have the rest which I am sure you well deserve.
I remain, etc.,
