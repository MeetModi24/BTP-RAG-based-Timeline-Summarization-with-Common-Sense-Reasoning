241. LETTER TO T. MORISON
[HOTEL CECIL,
LONDON,]
November 22, 1906
DEAR MR. MORISON,
I now enclose herewith a cutting with marked passages, giving the
utterances of Lord Selborne. One was before the war and the other
only lately.
I also send you copy of Sir Muncherji's letter to Mr. Lyttelton,
with the passages marked.
That we would be satisfied with legislation along the Cape lines is
made, you will notice, quite clear in the Representation to Lord Elgin of
which you have a copy and of which if you need I will send you more
copies. I hope you have noticed the fundamental objection to the Asiatic
Ordinance, namely, that it draws for the first time a colour line and it
means a departure from the Colonial tradition. If there was no hesitation
last year in vetoing the Native Land Tenure Bill, it passes comprehension

is infinitely worse than the Native Tenure Ordinance.
