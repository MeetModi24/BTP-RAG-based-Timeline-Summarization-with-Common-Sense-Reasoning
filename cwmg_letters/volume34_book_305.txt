305. LETTER TO RAMDAS GANDHI
August 11, 1927
You have no reason at all to be anxious on account of Kanti-
lal. You must have known of the step I have taken. I have
already written to you that you should never worry about a matter
which does not concern you. A further hint we can read in yet
another verse: "therefore what is unavoidable thou shouldst not
regret".
You will find this verse in the second chapter. It means, you
should not grieve over that which you cannot control. Thus the
Lord assuaged Arjuna's grief. Generally, we act contrary to this.
We weep when someone passes away. We are plunged in sorrow
if our house is burnt to ashes; the flood almost robbed us of our
sense, but death, fire, floods, etc., are inescapable. What is the
use of grieving over them? But this statement does not mean
that we should become hard-hearted. We should undertake such
remedies as we can to put out a fire, to retrieve the loss caused
by floods and to save the dying, but we should not let those events
affect us in any other way. Now I have to take it that you can-
not come here and that is right; but come when you are free and
feel like it. Just come to me wherever I might happen to be.
[From Gujarati]
From the manuscript of Mahadev Desai's Diary. Courtesy: Narayan Desai
