210. LETTER TO LORD AMPTHILL
[LONDON,]
August 10, 1909
MY LORD,
Mr. Hajee Habib and I have just returned from the interview with
Lord Crewe. His Lordship was very sympathetic; he gave a patient hear-
ing. I slightly sketched before him, as I saw that the opportunity was
too great to be missed, the amendment that I sent to you last evening.
I took the liberty of mentioning that we had discussed the question fully
with Your Lordship. Lord Crewe nodded appreciatingly and said that
you had taken great pains over the question. From what Lord Crewe
said, I imagine that the negotiations still continue. I think he admits
that the amendment that I have suggested is very reasonable and that he
would press it upon General Smuts. I do not know what should now be
done in the circumstances. I await Your Lordship's advice.
I remain, etc.,
