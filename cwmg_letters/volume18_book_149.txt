149. LETTER TO N. C. SINHA
BOLPUR,
[Before September 17, 1920]
DEAR MR. SINHA 3 ,
I am glad you have written about the lawyers. We shall never
fight our way to swaraj without taking any risks and without caus-
ing some disturbance in the ordinary life. I agree with you that
we lawyers have been the bete noir of the magistracy, but that was
when in their opinion, we caused the greatest trouble. But you
will see that when we ourselves abandon the courts, the process
will not be relished by the bureaucracy. What does it matter if
temporarily the Santhal Parganas and such other districts are depri-
ved of the assistance the lawyers have only just begun to render to
them, and I can conceive hundreds of ways of helping them with-
out pleading their cause before biased or ignorant magis-
trates. The lawyers today lead public opinion, and conduct all
political activity. This they do during the few leisure hours they
get from their tennis and billiards. I do not expect that by divid-
ing their leisure hours between billiards and politics, lawyers will
bring us substantially near swaraj. I want at least the public work-
ers among them to be whole-timers, and when that happy day
comes, I promise a different outlook before the country.
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
The Hindu, 29-9-1920
