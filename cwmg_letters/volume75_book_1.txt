1. LETTER TO AMRIT KAUR
Unrevised
October 11, 1941
MY DEAR IDIOT,
Ramsarandas is an old devotee of Ludhiana. He has been
regularly sending money and khadi. He has a doctor son in Eng-
land, another in India but married to [a] Scotch girl. She wrote
asking for permission for herself and Father-in-law to come and pass
a few days at Sevagram. He became ill and was nursed back to
health. His teeth were all extracted. They were the seat of disease.
Bessie is a fine steady girl, extremely devoted to her father-in-law,
nearly as old as I am. He insisted on doing C[ivil] D[isobedience].
He proposes to go again. He says he does not mind dying there.
My tour is off for the time being. For G. says he must try. If
he fails, I might be allowed. Of course he won't fail. I have told
you about my anxiety. It won't be any more for you than for any
other patient. But the point is not your anxiety or mine. It is
that you should not become ill. You should find out the art of
keeping well. You live on the border line. That is bad.
Love.
BAPU
[PS.]
Lilavati came in today. She has a month's leave.
