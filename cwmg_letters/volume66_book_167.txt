167. LETTER TO AMRIT KAUR
SEGAON, WARDHA,
September 15, 1937
MY DEAR AMRIT,
Bapu has given me many messages for you today.
1.
He wants you to move amongst the high-class Sikhs and awaken
their interest in the Prohibition campaign. Those who drink should be weaned
of it, and especially the women should be roused to draw up a resolution.
I would like a declaration from notable Sikhs.
2. C. F. A. should eat exactly what the doctor says. Probably his condi-
tion is one which can actually be harmed by fruit, etc. But now, of course,
he may [have] passed that stage. It should depend on what the doctor
says. That C. F. A. eats meat is open knowledge to Bapu.
3.
Bapu entirely agrees with you that C. F. A. should not think about
answering Miss Mayo's book. It is not needed either in India or the West.
An entirely independent book would be the only way of touching the matter. 2
4.
There is no likelihood of Bapu going to the Frontier before October.
It depends on when and what Khan Saheb 3 writes.
5.
It is very good news that the Simla Khadi Bhandar has become
self-supporting.
6.
Bapu will look at your brass bucket, and if it is suitable, he will
use it himself.
7.
Your fan is put away. There are plenty more here. Yours will be
kept for you to use when you return.
8.
Bapu's right hand is quite usable, but he feels the more rest he
can give it the better. And this, he points out, has the advantage of giving
him enforced rest from too much writing.
9.
Bapu thinks your Hindi letters have much improved, and they are
not too big now.
