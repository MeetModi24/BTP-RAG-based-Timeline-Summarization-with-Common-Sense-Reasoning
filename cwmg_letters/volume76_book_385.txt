385. LETTER TO T. B. SAPRU
SEVAGRAM, WARDHA, C.P.,
July 29, 1942
DEAR DR. SAPRU,
Nothing from your pen I can possibly disregard. If I had
the slightest faith in a Round Table Conference when British
authority reigns supreme, I would accept your proposal. You
know what happened when Sir Sankaran Nair presided, when I
presided in Delhi-was it not?-and in London when I was
thrust into the chair. Of course you are at liberty to say that
each time the fault was mine. My interpretation is different.
The result is the same. I am not the fit person for such a task.
But if you have faith and you call it, I shall be at your service.
You at least will acquit me of haste or pride. I have seen no-
thing impossible of acceptance in my or, rather, now, the Congress
demand.
I hope you have got rid of all your illness.
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
