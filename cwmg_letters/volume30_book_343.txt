343. LETTER TO LABHSHANKAR MEHTA
ASHRAM, SABARMATI,
Wednesday, April 14, 1926
BHAISHRI LABHSHANKAR,
1. The English maxim you quote applies generally in the case
of ailments. One learns mostly from experience where to apply
such maxims.
2. Never have I seen or heard of one becoming rich by sweat-
ing. There is however a saying that everyone should sweat to earn
his livelihood.
3. I do not think it is right to say that the principles pro-
pounded in Hind Swaraj are not workable just because I cannot
practise them perfectly. The maxim that you quote can certainly
not apply to me, because not only do I refuse to excuse myself, but
positively confess my shortcoming.
4. If you must make a distinction between a vow and a re-
solve, the vow is certainly worthier. It is a resolve that cannot
be given up. A resolve that can be is worthless.
5. I do not understand your fifth question. Is there really
any principle behind the Latin proverb you quote? What could it
mean?
6. I cannot appreciate the relationship that you describe.
7. I regard the study of astronomy as essential.
