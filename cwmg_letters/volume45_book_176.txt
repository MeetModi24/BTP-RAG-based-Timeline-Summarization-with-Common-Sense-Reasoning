176. LETTER TO JAISHANKAR TRIVEDI
January 27, 1931
BHAI TRIVEDI,
I was not happy that I had to come away without meeting
you or seeing Manu, but I was not free to decide my own plans.
I did receive your gift. I gave the vegetables to co-workers and
I am taking with me the tomatoes, dates and almonds. I write
this letter in the train. I am writing it for the sake of an English
prisoner named Wheeler. He served me in many ways while I
was in jail. He is an intelligent man and has great love for
animals. I liked him. He has no teeth, though he is only
middle-aged. I wish to help him to get a denture. I have
obtained Major Martin's permission for this. If you know any
person in Poona who makes good dentures, persuade him to do
this act of service. Or you yourself may bear the expense and get
a denture made for him. Perhaps Major Martin will not send
him there. In that case, take the doctor in your car to Yeravda.
Meet Quinn or the Major and do what is needed. Let me know
afterwards what you did. Ask Manu to write to me.
I got the books which you gave to Pyarelal.
Blessings from
BAPU
