83. LETTER TO SIR HENRY COTTON
HOTEL CECIL,
LONDON,
November 2, 1906
DEAR SIR HENRY,
Mr. Scott, Mr. Robertson, and Mr. Mackarness have suggested a
meeting of Members at the House to hear the views of the Indian Dele-
gates. Sir William approves of the idea. It seems to me that if such a
meeting were possible before the date fixed by Lord Elgin for meeting
1 Printers of India, who presumably did Gandhiji's printing work while he was
in England
the Deputation, and if a resolution were adopted by the meeting sympa-
thizing with the objects of the Deputation, it will strengthen the hands
of the Deputation, as also Lord Elgin's. I have therefore written to
Mr. Scott. If you approve of the idea, may I ask you to move in the
matter. If you desire me to wait upon you, I will do so with pleasure.
Yours truly,
SIR HENRY COTTON, M.P.
45, ST. JOHN'S WOOD PARK, N.W.
