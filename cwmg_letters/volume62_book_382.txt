382. LETTER TO AMRIT KAUR
WARDHA,
April 20, 1936
MY DEAR REBEL,
You are right in saying I worry about nothing and therefore
not even about your persistent cough which you so lightly regar-
ded. But not to worry is not to neglect or underrate such infor-
mation. Why should you be so delicate? Why should the sligh-
test change in the weather upset you? If it is the diet that is at
the bottom, you must revert to the old regimen. On no such ac-
count may you endanger your health. There are occasions in a
person's life when everything has to be sacrificed. Surely a new
regimen suddenly adopted is not such an occasion. My own in-
clination is always on the side of nature cure. But that you
may not adopt unless Shummy co-operates, you believe in it, and
you get a proper guide. A guide is obtainable but Shummy may
not agree and you may not have faith in it. Mere liking is of
no consequence. Please do not play with your ailments. Even a
rebel has to submit to the laws of nature.
I look forward to better news tomorrow.
You know all about the tragedy of the wire. You began to
crow before the dawn.
Love.
TYRANT
[PS.]
The electric stand adorns my desk now. Bharatan brought
it yesterday.
