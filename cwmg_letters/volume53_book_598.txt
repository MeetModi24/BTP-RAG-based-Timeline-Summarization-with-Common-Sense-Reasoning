598. LETTER TO M. R. JAYAKAR
March 2, 1933
DEAR MR. JAYAKAR,
I thank you for your letter. If you can give me your opi-
nion without much tax on your energy, I would value it. Every
1 Ada West
2 A letter, in terms identical with paragraph 3 onwards, was sent to

help that comes counts in the midst of the difficulties that are
being created day after day.
I hope you are fast regaining your lost strength.
I wonder if you have seen the Madras Law Review's opinion
on Rao Bahadur Rajah's Bill I enclose it for your perusal.
Tell me whether you still hold to your opinion.
I personally
think that Mr. Rajah's Bill deserves support.
You had my telegram in reply to yours. Whether for the
moment we succeed or not, we must continue to create public opi-
nion in favour of the Bills and make such effort as is possible to
have them passed. That we did not succeed on the 27th does
not worry me.
I hope you are following carefully what I am writing for
the Harijan. It is my weekly letter to you as much as it is to
other co-workers. You will read what I have said about temples.
Mathuradas must have gone to Benares to see Malaviyaji.
Yours sincerely,
SJT. M. R. JAYAKAR, BAR-AT-LAW
THE ASHRAM
WINTER ROAD
MALABAR HILL
