216. LETTER TO Y. M. PARNERKAR
NEW DELHI,
June 30, 1947
CHI. PARNERKAR 2 ,
I have your letter.
I am strongly against people going abroad. I cannot appre-
ciate the assumption that all knowledge is concentrated abroad
and only ignorance abounds at home.
Keep in mind that in the West they experiment on living
beings so that the human body may be preserved. In order to
extract all that they can from the cow they slaughter her as
well as the bull and feed on their flesh. What can we learn
from them?
But I am alone although my being alone does not hurt me.
What if I am in the wrong? Am I not confining the mistake
to myself? Therefore I have only one advice to offer, that you
should do what the Goseva Sangh suggests. But no going abroad
even if they suggest it. Serving the Goseva Sangh means
serving the cow because the former came into being only for
the latter's sake. Try to bring round your colleagues by your
knowledge.
I have no time or inclination to write more.
I hope Sharad is doing well.
Blessings from
BAPU
