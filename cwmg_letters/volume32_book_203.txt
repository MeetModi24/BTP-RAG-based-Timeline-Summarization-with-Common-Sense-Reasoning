203. LETTER TO MANIBEHN PATEL
[January 1, 1927]
CHI. MANI,
I have your letter. Read the letter on the reverse. I am
thinking of sending you for this work. Only you or Mirabehn can
work there. The girls there would be Sindhi and so knowledge
of English and Hindi would be necessary. We cannot send Mira-
behn as yet. I, therefore, should like you to go. If you decide
to do so let me know.
Whether you feel happy or not, you have to stay in the
Ashram, that is to say, with me. Pour out your heart to me and
exact from me a mother's duties. Are you sure that the reason
for your lack of interest is not the absence of a life-compa-
nion? A well-wisher of yours emphatically advised me that I
should get you married. The suggestion was made as we were
talking about a certain young man. He is not a Patidar, but is
a deserving youth. I said that I had no fear on your account.
I see no signs at present to suggest that you may wish to get
married. He then told me that I did not know you. You can see
from my language that I am not joking this time. Reply to me
without any fear. One thing is certain, that a girl who wants to
remain unmarried all her life should be brave. She should remain
cheerful otherwise people would say that she should be married off.
Blessings from
BAPU
CHI. MANIBEHN PATEL
SATYAGRAHA ASHRAM
SABARMATI
[From Gujarati]
Bapuna Patro—Manibehn Patelne, pp. 43-
