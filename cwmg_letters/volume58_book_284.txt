284. LETTER TO MATHURADAS TRIKUMJI
July 30, 1934
Your letter was all right. I got it only today. It was given
to me immediately after the meeting of the Working Committee
had ended.
There is an error in your suggestion, namely, that at present
we are not offering civil disobedience. Appointing Abdul Ghaffar
Khan would violate norms. Nobody has understood the difference
between the present programme and the previous one. Our actions
and words have become too arrogant. That is a sign of violence.
Not only do we lack the regard and courtesy which we should
show even towards an enemy, but I feel that falsehood and arro-
gance, instead of diminishing, are increasing among the workers.
This requires further explanation, but I don't have time for that
just now. If you wish to know the reasons, when we meet and if
I have time, you may ask me.
You also have been appointed one of the Treasurers. See
that you don't overtax your health. You can't afford to do that.
[From Gujarati]
Bapuni Prasadi, p. 149
