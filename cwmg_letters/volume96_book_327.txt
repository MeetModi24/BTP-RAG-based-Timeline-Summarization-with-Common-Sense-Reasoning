327. LETTER TO H. S. L. POLAK
August 30, 1941
MY DEAR HENRY,
You have missed the golden canon of interpretation. So long as
you can think of [one] favourable, never draw an unfavourable
interpretation. You say unconsciously I have taken up C.D. for the
sake of saving the Congress from disintegration. I suggest one most
natural to me. I deliberately undertook C.D. to preserve non-violence.
I joined the Congress for that purpose. I would leave it, if it gave
it up. I expect great things from the very mild form now going. Self-
restraint assists in the mildness. Your suggestion in the last para is
a bad hit so unworthy of you. But I must not wonder. Living as you
are in the midst of the inferno, you cannot view things otherwise than
you have done.
But I must not tax you any further. My whole heart is with you
all. I did shed a silent tear when I learnt of the damage to the Abbey,
the Houses of Parliament and the cathedral. I have not changed for
the worse, if I have changed at all. I have grown and am still growing.
With love to you all,
BHAI
