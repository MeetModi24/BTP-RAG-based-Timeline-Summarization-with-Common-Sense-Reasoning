318. LETTER TO H. S. L. POLAK
[LONDON,]
October 22, 1909
MY DEAR HENRY,
I am not going to write at length this week. You will see the copies
of various correspondence which shows that I have still to wait.
Mrs. Ritch has to undergo another serious operation next week.
Ritch is now practically fixed up here, possibly for good.
Mr. Doke's book cannot still be delivered. Poor Cooper is at his
wit's end. His printer being in gaol, the printer's wife has not been able
to keep her promise. Next week, I think, delivery will take place without
fail.
I am sending you one of the booklets of Tolstoy, which you should
read. I think it is very good.
Mr. Abdul Caadir leaves for South Africa tomorrow, so that now
there will be only Mr. Anglia and Mr. Hajee Habib here.
I enclose herewith Mr. Phelp's letter and a pamphlet addressed to
Mr. Roosevelt.
From a photostat of the typewritten office copy: S. N. 5144
