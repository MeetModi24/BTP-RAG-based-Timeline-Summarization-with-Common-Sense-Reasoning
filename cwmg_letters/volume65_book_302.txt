302. LETTER TO MANUBEHN S. MASHRUWALA
TITHAL,
June 5, 1937
CHI. MANUDI,
I got your letter. You haven't used your brains properly.
Why do you think that it was your aunt who tempted you or that
only Surendra could tempt you? As soon as you were separated
from me, you entered a vast field of temptations. You are a
very credulous, simple-hearted and weak girl. All of us are
so to some degree. That is why we voluntarily raise a hedge
in the form of rules round ourselves for our protection. I think
you have put up a few such hedges. I wish, and beg of you,
that you should never cross them, no matter who tempts you,
whether aunt or husband, mother, father or uncle, or an utter
stranger.
We are leaving this place on the 10th.
Have you followed my letter? Your sitar and shoes have
been sent to Bombay with Vasumati.
Blessings from
BAPU
From a photostat of the Gujarati: C.W. 1561. Courtesy: Manubehn S.
Mashruwala
