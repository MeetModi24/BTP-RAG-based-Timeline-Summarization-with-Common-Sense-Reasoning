251. LETTER TO B. K. DIWANJI
SEGAON, WARDHA, C . P.
August 4, 1936
DEAR DIWANJI,
It cannot be desirable for Indians to act as agents of
foreign concerns whose traffic is detrimental to the moral or mater-
ial interests of the country. This covers all your questions but
I may add that acceptance of advertisements, Indian or foreign,
whose object is to advertise things which are contrary to the policy
advocated by the newspapers cannot be defended.
The medical profession is a noble profession only when medi-
cal men practise it for service, not for money, and if they give
predominant place to prevention rather than cure, and accept the
limits to their remedial measures so that they are not contrary
to humanness towards non-human life.
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
From a copy: Pyarelal Papers: Courtesy: Pyarelal
