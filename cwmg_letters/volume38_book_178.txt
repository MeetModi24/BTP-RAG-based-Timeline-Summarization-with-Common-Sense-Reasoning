178. LETTER TO ASHRAM WOMEN
WARDHA,
Silence Day, December 3, 1928
SISTERS,
I have your letter written by Gangabehn. There is some
point in what you say about the noise. But the responsibility for
it lies not only with the children but also with the adults. Again,
it should not be difficult for you to observe silence or to make
the children observe silence while dining or working. The main
point is this: You should not think that if there is no talking,
time will hang heavily while dining or working; or that the child-
ren cannot be kept quiet. There are millions of men who do their
work quietly. You know, do you not?, that labourers in big fac-
tories are forced to keep silent while working.
Why cannot
we voluntarily do what they have to do under compulsion?
Hereafter Kakasaheb will be with you once a week. Do you
still insist on Valjibhai too coming there? If I press him, he will
come; but I know that he is always too busy and so, as a rule, I
do not like to put any more burden upon him, if I can help it.
Blessings from
BAPU
