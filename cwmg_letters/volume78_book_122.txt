122. LETTER TO NARANDAS GANDHI
BOMBAY,
September 10/12, 1944
CHI. NARANDAS,
I have your letter. I understand what you say. I like
your suggestion. Give on loan to the Kathiawar Khadi Mandal
the money not already invested. When a new association is form-
ed, it may make its own arrangement. Make some suggestions
regarding the new association. Consult friends there.
Janakibehn will not be able to go. Chi. Kamalnayan is
going with her message. Do not detain him for more than a day
or two. He is sparing time from an extremely tight schedule.
I hope your programme will succeed. If the programme does
not succeed so well there this time as in the previous years,
console yourself with the thought that it is your own venture
that has now become a country-wide movement. If, therefore,
your little rivulet, which was the source, looks small in comparison,
you should not feel bad about it. Whether I will live long
enough to see the National Fund put to good use or whether
others who follow me will manage it in a worthy manner, is
entirely in God's hands. For us one step is enough.
Blessings from
BAPU
[PS.]
September 12, 1944
On further reflection, I feel that our real money is neither
gold nor silver nor copper, but yarn. And at present we have
no rival in regard to that. How nice would it be if the quan-
tity this time is much larger than before! I should be happy if
in the end you become the first mint of yarn. Yarn is produced
at many places. But not all of them become mints. Think what
makes a mint what it is.
Blessings from
BAPU
