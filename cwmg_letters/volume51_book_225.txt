225. LETTER TO E. E. DOYLE
September 27, 1932
DEAR COL. DOYLE,
The substance of what I said to you yesterday before break-
ing my fast was this. I was breaking the fast not without mis-
givings in that the British Government's acceptance of the settle-
ment related only to the part that was included in the British
Government's communal decision.
This was perhaps natural.
But I was bound to make my position clear-that I would be in
honour bound to secure a due fulfilment of the whole of the
settlement. The second thing that I drew your attention to was
that I would expect Government to continue the facilities given
to me since 20th for seeing friends and sending messages in the
matter of the untouchability question. The British acceptance has
reference only to the political part of the settlement, but the most
vital part for the whole of the Hindu community is that relating to
social and religious reform. In order to set it on a firm footing it
is necessary for me to be in touch with the workers for some time to
come. In order to avoid any misunderstanding therefore I men-
tioned this very important thing to you before breaking the fast.
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
