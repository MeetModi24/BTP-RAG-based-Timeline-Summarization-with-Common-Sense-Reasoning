189. LETTER TO MRS. S. WALTON
[HOTEL CECIL,
LONDON,]
November 16, 1906
DEAR MRS. WALTON,
I have just been talking to a valued friend about the conversation
we had yesterday. He is a missionary belonging to the Arya Samaj
of the Punjab. The Samaj is to Hinduism what Protestantism is to
Catholicism. The missionary friend is under a vow of poverty and his
talents are devoted to the work of education combined with religion.
He is an M.A. of Punjab University but in order to increase his use-
fulness he is staying in London and studying for the London M.A. I
suggested to him that, if he could enter a quiet, nice English home, he
would be able to see the real culture and beauty of English life which
would be of immense use in his work. At the same time he is to live
as cheaply as possible. Do you know a home that would take him without
pecuniary consideration? Of course he would pay for his board and
lodging but he cannot pay more than £1 per week. It does not matter

where such a home is so long as it enables him to reach the British Museum
within half an hour or at the most three quarters of an hour.
I am,
Yours sincerely,
MRS. SPENCER WALTON
ANDREW HOUSE
TONBRIDGE
