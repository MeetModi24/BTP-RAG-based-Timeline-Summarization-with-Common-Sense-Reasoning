241. LETTER TO THE PRESS
SABARMATI,
January 10, 1920
TO
THE EDITOR
THE [BOMBAY] CHRONICLE
[BOMBAY]
SIR,
Mr. Andrews cabling from Mombasa says :
Full draft proposed ordinance menacing Indian political freedom
reads thus:
"Firstly, the Bill may be cited as Removal of Undesirables Ordinance,
servility. He has dared to claim an equal status with the recently
arrived European settler. He has dared to dispute the haughty
position taken up by his European rival in trade. The latter has
therefore captured the executive. The East African position admits
of no compromise such as was possible and perhaps necessary in
South Africa where the condition was totally different from the
East African condition. The Indian adds the right of priority to
the ordinary natural right to live on terms of equality with every
other kind of settler in East Africa. And I venture to hope that
his claim will be universally supported in India for the preser-
vation of his full political and municipal status, and that the
Government of India will exercise to the full its undoubted right
and perform the duty of protecting the British Indian settler in
East Africa.
Yours, etc.,
M. K. GANDHI
The Bombay Chronicle, 12-1-1920
