175. LETTER TO MARGARETE SPIEGEL
July 9, 1934
CHI. AMALA,
I have your letter. You must not be so thin-skinned. And
why should you mind what a mere youngster says? He may say
what he likes. You should simply ignore what is useless and
take in what is useful. Puratan is a good young man. You should
take in good part what he says. In one thing you are decidedly
wrong, i.e., in imposing your will upon the children. You can
only make them do things that they do willingly. Your ten years'
bad practice cannot become good by prescription. But I cannot
have you to impose things on children, nor will I impose anything
on you. If you are finding life irksome there, tell me what you will
have me to do. You have to settle down to something. Think
hard and guide me.
Love.
BAPU
[PS.]
Yes, I have been taking milk for the last four days.
