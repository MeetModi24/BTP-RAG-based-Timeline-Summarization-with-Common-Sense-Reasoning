11. LETTER TO J. B. KRIPALANI
February 24, 1947
This is a very personal letter but not private.
Manu Gandhi my grand-daughter, as we consider blood-
relations, shares the bed with me, strictly as my very blood . . .5
as part of what might be my last yajna. This has cost me dear-
est associates. . . . You as one of the dearest and earliest com-
rades. . . should reconsider your position in the light of what
they have to say. . . . I have given the deepest thought to the
matter. The whole world may forsake me but I dare not
leave what I hold is the truth for me. It may be a delusion
and a snare. If so, I must realize it myself. I have risked per-
dition before now. Let this be the reality if it has to be.
I need not argue the point. I have simply conveyed the
intensity of my thoughts.
I suggest your discussing it with X and Y. And then come
to a conclusion and let me know. . . . Do not consider my feel-
ings in the matter. I have none. All I want is to do the truth
at all cost, as I see it.
Mahatma Gandhi—The Last Phase, Vol. I, Bk. II, pp. 220-
