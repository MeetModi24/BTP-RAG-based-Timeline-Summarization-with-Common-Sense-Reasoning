398. LETTER TO W. WEDDERBURN
[JOHANNESBURG,]
July 30, 1906
SIR WILLIAM WEDDERBURN, BARONET
84- PALACE CHAMBERS
LONDON, ENGLAND
DEAR SIR,
I have just returned from the front. Although I cannot now be of
any use in connection with the Report of the Constitution Committee,
it is still the opinion of the British Indian Association that I should pro-
ceed to England accompanied by one or two merchants, not necessarily
to influence the letters patent that will be granted but to place the British
Indian position personally before the authorities. I shall, therefore, be
obliged if you will kindly let me know by cable whether such a deputation
is likely to be of any use whatsoever. If it is considered unnecessary, the
word "needless" would explain the meaning. If you think that a depu-
tation such as the above should proceed, please cable "yes". I do not
know whether the end of September or October will be a suitable time.
I remain,
Yours truly,
