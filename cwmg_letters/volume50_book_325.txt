325. LETTER TO CHHAGANLAL P. MEHTA
August 4, 1932
Your conduct henceforth should show to others that you
understand the true significance of Doctor's death. His many
virtues are his real will. They are your legacy. I hope you will
do nothing which may make your younger brothers unhappy in
the slightest degree. . . .3 I feel unhappy that, at a time when my
lifelong friend has passed away, I am as helpless as a cripple.
If I had been free, I would be by your side now. Perhaps Doctor
would have drawn his last breath in my lap. But God is ever
jealous of our wishes. I must, therefore, content myself with what
I can do through letters.
[From Gujarati]
Mahadevbhaini Diary, Vol. I, p. 343
