118. LETTER TO G. D. BIRLA
[October 18, 1933]
BHAI GHANSHYAMDAS,
Despite all intentions of writing to you I have been unable
to do so till today. Jamnalal shields me well from the visitors
but who can protect me from letters? Never am I able to clear
my desk. Because the order of going to bed early has also to
be observed I am writing this having got up at 3 a.m. The im-
plication is not that this letter is the sole cause for rising early.
Even during the time allowed in the evening I am prevented from
doing anything substantial by the pestering insects.
I have read your article about Jawaharlal. It is good and
you have done no harm by writing it. There could be no need
to suppress our opinions about one another. When Truth alone
is to be sought, suppression of one's opinion becomes a fault. You
must have sent the article to Jawaharlal, if not, do so. He is a
very straightforward man and corrects his error. I am confident
ultimately he is bound to follow the path of Truth. And if his
line of thinking proves to be correct, comments would be super-
fluous. Equality can never mean uniformity. Equality only means
uniformity in justice. There is no distinction between an atom
and the Himalayas in the eyes of God. He is the same to the
atom as to the Himalayas.
Gopi left yesterday. I could not have a long talk with her
but she used to come and sit with me every day. She is an
extremely simple girl and was very happy here; she mixed with
everybody. She went to Bombay on account of Diwali. She was
also keen to see the illuminations there. It will be good if she
returns after Diwali. I have no doubt that she will be ready.
Gajanan has replied to the letter I wrote to him. Gopi too was
asked to write a letter. Write to me about your health.
Blessings from
BAPU
