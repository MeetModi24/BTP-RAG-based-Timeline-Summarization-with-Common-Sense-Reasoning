455. LETTER TO KANU GANDHI
PATNA,
May 18, 1947
CHI. . . . ,
I saw this letter among the papers I have taken up after
prayers. You have asked me to send a telegram about the
photographer. Your letter is dated April 25. Manu did tell
me something about it. After coming here I have just taken
up the pending papers. I am able to attend to your letter
only today, and so, I am not sending you a telegram. I am
not so enthusiastic about the photographer. But I do not wish
to disappoint you, hence, I leave it to you.
People have built roads with their own labour and, if the
Government does not pay them wages, I don't think there is
anything wrong if as an exception we pay. For, they would
have certainly expected payment in this case. But we cannot
create a precedent like that.
It will do if you show this letter to . . . . However, I will
write a separate letter or dictate one.
Most probably I have written to you about. . . . I have been
a little worried about . . . after . . .'s letter. And I cannot say that
I approved of. . .'s suggestion that . . . should stay only with you.
But I have accepted the suggestion. I did not like it because it
was contrary to what I had decided. Both of you had accepted
it, too. But I consider . . . a mere child, and hence, if she cannot
bear the life there, she should not be forced to live there. But
you alone can decide about it. I can only say that you have
my permission. And . . . ought to improve her health. How is
she going to serve, if she does not improve her health?
I shall probably leave here on the 24th for Delhi. Manu has
been operated upon for appendicitis. You must have known it
already. She is still in the hospital. She is all right. It is terribly
hot here. During these months it is bound to be hot everywhere in
India except on the hills. I have so far not found the heat
unbearable.
[From Gujarati]
Biharni Komi Agman, pp. 378-
