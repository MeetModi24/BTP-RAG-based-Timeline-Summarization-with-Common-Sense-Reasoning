176. LETTER TO G. M. THENGE
January 24, 1933
DEAR FRIEND,
I have your letter. My statement has not the meaning that
you have assigned to it, as would be quite clear to you if you will
read it as a whole. I have considered myself to be incapable of
coming to a judgment one way or the other, if only because I
have not all the data for coming to a judgment. I have there-
fore stated in unequivocal language that every Congressman has
to decide for himself or herself, taking stock of the circumstances
over which they alone have control and I have none, as also of
their own individual obligations of which they alone can have
knowledge and I can have none.
Yours sincerely,
SJT. G. M. THENGE
PAREKH STREET, GIRGAUM
BOMBAY
