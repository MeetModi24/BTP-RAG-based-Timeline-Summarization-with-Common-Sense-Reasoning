37. LETTER TO N. S. VARADACHARI
THE ASHRAM,
SABARMATI,
June 22, 1926
MY DEAR VARADACHARI,
Your letter has much relieved me. I know that your reso-
lution will remove all your difficulties. The question of incre-
ment is a mere detail. You will not be alarmed at the manner
in which I have discussed the problem arising from letters like
yours. There has been a crop of such letters recently from several
parts of India. I thought, therefore, that I would gently discuss
the problem in the pages of Young India.
I have seen C.R.'s letter to you. I saw it only yesterday,
Shankerlal being in Bombay. The thought never grasped my
mind that Ramanathan's case and the increase given to him had
anything to do with you. On the contrary, Shankerlal told me
that your pecuniary difficulty arose, or at least came to his notice,
earlier than Ramanathan's. And in any case, I have too great
regard for you even to suspect that you would want to take an
improper advantage of any situation.
Yours sincerely,
SJT. N. S. VARADACHARI
