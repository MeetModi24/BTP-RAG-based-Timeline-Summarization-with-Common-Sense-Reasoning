133. LETTER TO SIR STAFFORD CRIPPS
SEVAGRAM, via WARDHA (C. P.),
August 7, 1946
DEAR SIR STAFFORD,
I have before me your two kind letters. Of course both
will be treated as private and confidential, though you have
marked one as personal and the other, received only two days
ago, as private and confidential.
I see from Reuter's telegrams that your health has again
given way and you have been obliged to take long rest. I am
sorry and hope you are listening to your medical advisers and
not wearing yourself out. I have come to know you and of
you more through your good wife than through anyone else.
I understand all you say about your and your colleagues'
attitude. I have always justified it from your own standpoint.
Therefore, so long as that trust continues, you will find me
defending you. The testing time is coming. I shall watch what
you will do in view of the latest Muslim League decision.
With regards to you both and all good wishes,
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
SIR STAFFORD CRIPPS
LONDON
Gandhiji's Correspondence with the Government, 1944-47, p. 216
