237. LETTER TO NATWARLAL
July 19, 1932
DEAR NATWARLAL,
Your letter has been received. It is very good that you have
been plying the takli. To ply the takli does not mean drawing out
a thread. But it is drawing 160 yards of yarn of at least 12 counts
of uniform twist per hour so that there may be no difficulty in
weaving. One should not have even any confidence about
letters from one in jail. Your letter came to my hand today,
that is, fourteen days after you wrote it. It remains to be seen
when this reaches you. In these circumstances there is no mean-
ing in my replying by return of post. But your vow is such that you
must have adhered to it on the ground of not receiving my letter.
This will certainly do you good. And if you can pull on without
tea and sugar for so many days, how can you not do without
them for ever? We know from the lives of crores (of people) in
this world that both these things are unnecessary, inasmuch as they
are acquainted neither with tea nor sugar. About jail life: the life
of one in jail cannot be written at length. But to the prisoners
whose mind is innocent this life happens to be peaceful and ele-
vating.
Blessings from
BAPU
Bombay Secret Abstracts, Home Department, Special Branch, File No.
