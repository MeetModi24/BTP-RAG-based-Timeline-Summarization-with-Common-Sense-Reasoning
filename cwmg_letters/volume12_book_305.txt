305. LETTER TO G. K. GOKHALE
PHOENIX,
NATAL,
April 1, 1914
DEAR MR. GOKHALE,
I had your two cables. To the last one I am just now replying.
I doubt whether Mrs. Gandhi will survive the settlement.
I am writing this by her bed side. I have to be her doctor, nurse
and everything. Then my brother's death leaves the sole charge of
five widows and their children in my hands. Dr. Mehta is just
now paying the expenses of the others. To this he will, I do not
doubt, add the maintenance of my brother's widow. But she and
the others are most naturally anxious to have me with them at
the earliest possible moment. I would, therefore, grudge having
to go to London unless you consider it absolutely necessary. If you
do, I shall certainly come if Mrs. Gandhi dies or is better— so well,
that is to say, as to permit of my being away from her, I suppose,
at least for two months.
As I have already informed you, Indian legislation will not
be undertaken before the reopening of the Union Parliament on
the 22nd instant.
I do hope that you will materially benefit by the cure at Vichy.
I know it is useless my asking you not to worry about me or
my future. Wherever I may be placed in India I shall carry out
my compact with you, viz., that I should observe absolute silence
except on the S[outh] A[frica] question for one year at least after
my landing in India and about everything else I have promised.
I am,
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
