598. LETTER TO LILAVATI ASAR
YERAVDA MANDIR,
October 27, 1932
CHI. LILAVATI,
I have your letter. I wrote what I wrote to satisfy you,
but I see that you are not satisfied. My intention in writing
that you were free was that nobody could force you, but you
could do as you wished. No other meaning was implied. I have
actually said that I have trust in you and that ultimately you
will attain stability. There have been numerous complaints.
They are all about your impatience, your indecision and your
anger. But I have not heeded them. I did not mean to suggest
that you had no respect for Narandas. But my words can be
interpreted to suggest that in spite of your respect for Narandas
you are unable to follow his instructions. And if you are unable
to do so, I see nothing wrong in your considering yourself free.
I wish you would give up your craving for a bicycle. You
should do whatever you can without it and be content.
I have forgotten what clarification you wanted in regard to un-
touchability. The other question can be answered from here itself.
BAPU
