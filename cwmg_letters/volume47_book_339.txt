339. LETTER TO A. SUBBIAH
P. O. Box 26,
AHMEDABAD,
August 16, 1931
MY DEAR SUBBIAH,
I have your letter. Your telegram was attended to as soon
as it was received. Now that I am not going to London I expect
to be relieved of some pressure. But it is also likely that the pressure
may increase.
Lalita has no excuse for not completing her Hindi. She can't
plead want of time nor want of tuition. You can give it to her
for a few minutes daily, but what she needs is not so much tuition
as application. I cannot help the feeling that if those from the
South who are nearest me will not take the trouble of learning
Hindi, there is something wrong, and then I have no right to
expect the others to learn it. Either a knowledge of Hindi is a
necessity for the service of the masses and therefore should be
at once learnt by all true servants or it is not necessary, in which
case the Hindi Prachar Office should be wound up. There is some-
thing untrue in the present vagueness. Let Lalita therefore beware.
I hope to be here for three weeks. . . .2
