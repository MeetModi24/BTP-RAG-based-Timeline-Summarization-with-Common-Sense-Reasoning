38. LETTER TO SIR WILLIAM WEDDERBURN
March 3, 1915
DEAR SIR WILLIAM 1 ,
The national calamity that has befallen us has prevented me
from replying earlier to your letter.
Many caskets of the kind sent to you have been brought to
India. But the Cape Town one was, as it were, destined to go to
England. It was the last one received in South Africa just on the day
of embarkation for Southampton. Then we stayed in London and
the thought of leaving the casket in England was developed during
the time. Mr. Roberts, Mr. Kallenbach and I agreed that the
best institution where it could be housed was your own house.
It
will only be a small personal tribute to one whom I have been
taught to love and regard as one of India's best friends. The de-
positing of the casket with you may serve as an effort in binding
the two nations together.
May I therefore ask you kindly to keep it yourself. I am sure
that had Mr. Gokhale read your letter he would have joined me in
my request.
With respects,
I remain,
Yours sincerely,
M. K. GANDHI
